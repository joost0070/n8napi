## 2. How exactly does the model work (stock, freight, lead time, samples, returns, QC, breakage)?

### Takeaway
Bloom Build lists tiles that sit in Foshan factories' stock as "In Stock". The customer pays the full amount at checkout. The factory releases the goods (~3 days), they go by **sea freight (2–4 weeks)**, and they are delivered to the kerb in Australia. A product page viewed on 4 Oct 2026 promised "Delivered Approx Fri 13 Nov", **about 5.5–6 weeks**. **I found no evidence of an "air freight first, then sea container" phase.** The current published model is ship-to-order by sea from day one. The only fast physical product is samples (3 for A$5), which must be stocked in Australia.

### Cited Findings
- Product page (Bicheno French Pattern Travertine Look, A$33/m², "vs retail showroom avg. $146/m²"): stock label "In Stock". Timeline shown: "Order Placed Today → Factory pick-up 3 days → Sea freight 2-4 week → Delivered Approx Fri 13 Nov" (seen 4 Oct 2026). Also "Your tiles ship direct from the same factories used by major retailers", "Damaged tiles replaced free", "10-year warranty" [FACT that the site says this] — [Product page](https://buybloombuild.com/products/bicheno-french-pattern-travertine-look-beige-matte-tile)
- Refund policy: "Once we have sent your shipping confirmation, your order is on the water and cannot be recalled, so any change-of-mind cancellation from that point attracts a **$250 restocking fee**". **No change-of-mind returns.** Customers are pushed to buy samples first [FACT, policy] — [Refund policy](https://buybloombuild.com/policies/refund-policy)
- Other refund-policy terms: damaged tiles are replaced free if reported **within 7 days** of delivery with photos, usually without return. Wrong or missing items must be reported within 14 days. Faults are handled under Australian Consumer Law (major fault means the customer chooses). Installed, cut or sealed tiles cannot be returned. Refunds take 3–10 business days [FACT, policy] — [Refund policy](https://buybloombuild.com/policies/refund-policy)
- FAQ: delivery is **to the kerb only**, where the driver unloads at the kerb or driveway. Shipping cost comes from a postcode calculator at checkout. The site recommends adding 15% for waste and rounds up to whole boxes (floor tile boxes are 20–30 kg). It sells "first-grade only… no seconds, factory rejects or B-grade", and "every order undergoes quality inspection before shipment". Payment is by card and standard checkout options (no deposit model visible; full payment up front) [FACT, site] — [FAQ](https://buybloombuild.com/pages/faq)
- Samples: "Order 3 samples for just $5", "Try before you buy" [FACT, site] — [Home](https://buybloombuild.com/); [FAQ](https://buybloombuild.com/pages/faq)
- Published unit cost breakdown (search snippet attributed to his LinkedIn and site; units presumably per m², though the snippet says "per tile"): materials $9.69 + sourcing & QC $4.25 + **sea freight $2.71** + customs $1.33 + **warehousing & breakage $2.50** + payment processing $1.19 = $21.67, plus a "flat 20% margin" = **$26** customer price [CLAIM] — [LinkedIn snippet](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jack-bloomfield/); [buybloombuild.com](https://buybloombuild.com/)
- Founder's framing: a tile customers pay ~$70/m² for "left the factory for less than $10". The markup goes to showroom rent, distributor warehouses and importer risk [CLAIM] — [LinkedIn HK](https://hk.linkedin.com/in/jack-bloomfield)
- Landing page splits customers into "Developer — Trade & volume" and "Renovator — Owner builder", with the tagline "Direct from the factory floor" [FACT, site] — [lander.buybloombuild.com](https://lander.buybloombuild.com/)

### Inferences
- **Cash conversion:** the customer pays 100% up front, about 5–6 weeks before delivery. If Bloom Build pays the factory on or after pickup (common for factory stock; terms unknown), working capital is **negative**. Customer cash funds goods and freight, there is no stock risk, and the only stock held locally is samples. This is the core of the model [INFERENCE].
- "In Stock" means *in the factory's stock*. Listing factory-held SKUs (standard Foshan collections, renamed with Australian place names like Bicheno, Roebuck and Pemberton) avoids MOQs and design risk [INFERENCE; the renaming is visible on the site].
- Individual orders (often a few m² to a few hundred m²) are far too small for an FCL. They are probably consolidated into LCL/groupage through a Foshan consolidator, then de-vanned at an Australian 3PL and sent out by pallet freight. The "warehousing & breakage $2.50" line points to an Australian cross-dock or 3PL, not a showroom [INFERENCE].
- The cost stack has **no line for customer acquisition, Australian last-mile delivery (charged separately), staff or overhead**. A "flat 20% margin" (a markup on cost, about 16.7% of the selling price) therefore has to cover all marketing and fixed costs. That is thin for paid-social D2C unless delivery fees and samples add margin, or the real gap to the A$33 price is larger. The example $26 vs the A$33 site price suggests margins vary by SKU [INFERENCE].
- **For bed linen in NL:** linen is light and high in value per kg, so air or courier (B2C parcel direct from China) is far more viable than for tiles. That makes an "air first, sea once volume is proven" path realistic for textiles even though Bloom Build does not use it. But see the EU €3 duty below and the much shorter delivery expectations in NL (next-day culture through bol.com/Coolblue) [INFERENCE].

## 5. Risks, what went wrong, criticism

### Takeaway
No Bloom Build-specific failures or criticism found. Structural risks are visible in the policies: a 5–6 week wait, no change-of-mind returns, a A$250 cancellation fee after dispatch, and kerb-only delivery. The founder's past opacity is the main reputational flag. The clearest cautionary comparable is **Made.com**.

### Cited Findings
- 2021 journalist scepticism: he refused to disclose store names, and sold courses alongside — [Boss Hunting](https://www.bosshunting.com.au/hustle/jack-bloomfield-interview)
- Strict policies (no change-of-mind returns, $250 restocking fee once "on the water", 7-day damage claim window) — [Refund policy](https://buybloombuild.com/policies/refund-policy)
- **Made.com (UK, comparable model):** founded 2010. Made-to-order from mostly Asian factories with multi-week lead times kept working capital lean. It reached £100m revenue in 2017 and £315m in FY2020. It listed in June 2021 at a £775m market cap. In December 2021, £45m of revenue was deferred because of supply-chain delays. Moving to hold stock and build more warehousing to cut lead times tied up cash. EBITDA guidance swung to a £50–70m loss. Trading was suspended in November 2022, and Next bought the brand/IP for £3.4m. About 12,000 UK orders were stuck in production or in Asia — [Flinder](https://www.flinder.co/insights/made-com-what-went-wrong); [Raconteur](https://www.raconteur.net/economy-trends/made-com-collapse-asos-ocado-retail); [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/24-30-october-2022/online-retailer-made-collapse/)

### Inferences
- Key risks if you copy the model: (1) a freight or port shock stretches 5 weeks into 10+ weeks, causing chargebacks and reputation damage; (2) customer cash is collected before delivery, so if the business fails, customers lose money (as happened at Made.com), which creates regulatory and consumer-trust exposure; (3) the transition to local inventory is the dangerous phase (Made.com died in it); (4) commoditised factory SKUs invite copycats [INFERENCE].
- EU-specific: under EU consumer law there is a **14-day withdrawal right for distance sales**, and bed linen is not exempt the way custom goods are. A "no change-of-mind returns" policy like Bloom Build's is **not legal in NL/EU** for standard products. Returns of textiles shipped from China are costly. This is a key difference from the Australian tile case [INFERENCE based on general knowledge of Directive 2011/83/EU; no source fetched here, so verify].

### Gaps
- No customer reviews, complaints or regulator actions on Bloom Build found.

## 6. Comparable operators and general data on factory-direct / pre-order D2C

### Takeaway
The most relevant proof that factory-direct works at scale in bed linen is **Quince** (US, "manufacturer-to-consumer", >US$1bn revenue in 2025, $10.1bn valuation in 2026). The main cautionary tale is **Made.com**. Consumer-tolerance data mostly covers 2–7 days. Multi-week waits are accepted only when they are clearly communicated and paid for with a large price gap.

### Cited Findings
- **Quince:** an M2C model in which "factories produce inventory on demand and ship their goods directly to consumers' doorsteps". Categories include cashmere, silk, **bedding**, furniture and travel. Revenue was >US$1bn in 2025, with triple-digit annual growth since its 2018 founding. It raised a US$500m Series E at a US$10.1bn valuation in March 2026 — [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/03/16/quince-secures-500-million-manufacturer-to-consumer-platform/); [WWD](https://wwd.com/business-news/financial/quince-10-1-billion-valuation-500-million-series-e-1238663323/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quince_(company))
- **Made.com:** see section 5 (batch made-to-order, which collapsed after moving into inventory) — [Flinder](https://www.flinder.co/insights/made-com-what-went-wrong)
- **Consumer tolerance:** McKinsey (via Shopifreaks) found 90% of consumers will wait 2–3 days and >80% will wait 4–7 days to avoid shipping fees. 22% abandon carts because of long delivery times. Home and furniture has about 80% cart abandonment (US-centric data) — [Shopifreaks/McKinsey](https://www.shopifreaks.com/mckinsey-survey-finds-90-of-consumers-will-wait-2-3-days-for-delivery-to-avoid-shipping-costs-as-e-commerce-growth-slows/); [Drip](https://www.drip.com/blog/cart-abandonment-statistics); [Red Stag](https://redstagfulfillment.com/percentage-of-online-shoppers-abandon-their-cart/)
- **Pre-order cash models:** the general argument is that pre-order or made-to-order shifts inventory risk and improves cash conversion, at the cost of lead time (vendor blogs, low evidentiary weight) — [EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/apparel-preorder-cash-model); [Portless](https://www.portless.com/blogs/how-to-implement-made-to-order-ecommerce-and-eliminate-q4-inventory-risk)
- **EU regulatory change relevant to direct B2C parcels from China:** from **1 July 2026** the EU removed the €150 customs-duty de minimis. A temporary **€3 duty per customs declaration line** (per tariff line, not per parcel) applies to B2C consignments of ≤€150. It is intended to last until about 1 July 2028, after which the standard tariffs apply. Import VAT already applied (IOSS) — [DHL](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-global/logistics-advice/import-export-advice/prepare-your-business-for-vat-de-minimis-removal); [Gerlach](https://gerlach-customs.com/knowledge-base/eu-parcel-duty-150-exemption/); [Baker Tilly](https://bakertillysee.com/insights/major-eu-customs-update-e3-duty-on-low-value-e-commerce-imports-starting-july-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Translating the model to Benelux bed linen:**
  - Bloom Build's price gap (around 50–77% below showroom) works because Australian tile retail carries heavy showroom, distributor and importer layers, and buyers compare per m². Mid-to-premium bed linen in NL already faces D2C and marketplace competition (online brands, bol.com, Amazon.nl, and Temu/Shein for the low end), so the headline gap will be smaller [INFERENCE].
  - The 5–6 week sea model clashes with Dutch next-day expectations. For textiles, direct air or courier B2C (about 7–12 days) is the more likely starting point, now with the €3 per-line duty, VAT and higher freight per unit. Sea plus an EU 3PL then follows once SKUs are proven. This is the "air first, then sea" sequence the user described, but as a design for NL, not as Bloom Build's actual practice [INFERENCE].
  - The mandatory 14-day EU withdrawal right removes Bloom Build's "no returns" protection. Return logistics to China are unfeasible, so an EU returns address or 3PL is needed from day one [INFERENCE].
  - Copy the A$5 sample mechanic as a fabric-swatch kit, and copy the cost-transparency positioning. Avoid the Made.com trap by moving to local stock only per SKU, once there is a demand history [INFERENCE].
- For quality-sensitive mid-to-premium textiles, claims such as OEKO-TEX, GOTS, thread count and fibre origin need certificates per batch. Factory-stock items often lack EU-relevant documentation, which makes the "sourcing & QC" cost line proportionally larger than for tiles [INFERENCE].

### Gaps
- No hard public data on refund or chargeback rates for multi-week pre-order D2C, or on customer tolerance for 2–4+ week delivery specifically in NL/EU.
- No specific bedding operator found that runs a factory-stock-listing plus sea-on-order model into the EU or Australia (other than Quince's US M2C model). Australian bed-linen brands (e.g. Bed Threads, Sheet Society) and EU ones were not researched in this pass.
- Standard EU import duty for bed linen (HS 6302) after 2028, and Dutch textile EPR (UPV Textiel) obligations, were not sourced here. These should be checked separately.
